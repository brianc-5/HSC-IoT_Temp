How the system works

Three independent parts, each with one job. They talk to each other in a fixed, well-defined way, so any part can be improved without breaking the others.

Stage 1 · Sensor Solar-powered sensor A small, low-power device that wakes at a set interval, measures temperature, humidity, light level and the voltage of its energy store, broadcasts one short radio message, and goes back to sleep. It detects its own hardware and power source automatically, so nothing ever needs to be configured by hand.
Stage 2 · Receiver USB-powered receiver Listens continuously for the sensor's broadcasts, filters out duplicate or implausible readings, keeps accurate time, and safely stores the newest 4,096 readings in memory that survives power loss. It shares them with your phone over Bluetooth while it keeps listening.
Stage 3 · This page Web app Connects to the receiver over Bluetooth, checks that the two are compatible, sets the receiver's clock, downloads the stored readings, and calculates every chart and estimate right here in your browser. Your measurements never leave your phone.

What each reading contains

Every reading stores the time it was taken, a sequence number, temperature, voltage, light level, humidity, radio signal strength, and a short description of the sensor's setup. If a measurement could not be taken, it is marked as unavailable rather than recorded as zero, so a failed sensor never invents a value.

The receiver keeps the newest 4,096 readings, so its history spans 4096 × sample interval: about 22.8 hours when sampling every 20 seconds, or about 8.5 days when sampling every 3 minutes.

Time-keeping

The receiver only counts time since it was switched on until a phone connects and sets its clock; earlier readings from the current session are then corrected to real time. Readings whose true time can no longer be established are reported as having an unknown time rather than being given a guessed one.

Each reading is identified by its time and sequence number, so downloading the history again never duplicates or overwrites older data.

The maths behind the numbers

Everything in the Power view is an estimate built on a stated assumption. Each model below explains in plain terms what it assumes, and when it refuses to produce a number rather than show a misleading one.

1 · Net power into storage

The sensor stores its energy in a supercapacitor, a small rechargeable energy store. The power flowing in or out of it follows from its stored energy E = ½·C·V²:

P_net(t) = C · V(t) · dV/dtC = storage capacitance (F, your setting) V = stored energy voltage at the sample (V) Positive P_net means the store is charging; negative means it is draining.

Comparing just two neighbouring samples would amplify measurement noise. The rate of change is instead taken as the slope of a least-squares line fitted over a trailing window of samples:

dV/dt = Σ(tᵢ − t̄)(Vᵢ − V̄) ⁄ Σ(tᵢ − t̄)²Window = your trailing window setting, default 45 min. Spacing comes from the actual reading timestamps, never from an assumed interval.

Refuses to produce a value when: the window holds fewer than 6 valid samples, or there is a large gap inside it. A fit spanning an outage would otherwise report a trend across data that does not exist.

Which readings count: only readings with a valid storage-voltage measurement are used. Readings taken while on USB power, and implausible values, are excluded rather than rescaled.

2 · Power use in darkness

The energy store only ever shows the net of solar energy coming in and the sensor's own consumption going out. In darkness, however, no solar energy comes in, so whatever the store loses is the system's own consumption:

P_drain ≈ median( −P_net )…over every estimate taken when the measured light level was below a threshold. The median is used, not the mean, so a few odd samples cannot skew the result.

The difficulty is choosing what counts as "dark". Too generous and residual daylight leaks in; too strict and there is not enough data. Rather than fixing one threshold, the page evaluates three (below 1, 3 and 5 lux) and checks whether neighbouring thresholds agree:

|D_high − D_low| ≤ max(5 µW, 20 % · D_low)D_low, D_high = consumption estimated with the stricter and the looser threshold of a neighbouring pair. If they agree, the stricter one is accepted. Agreement between two differently chosen thresholds is good evidence that neither is affected by stray light.

Each candidate threshold needs at least 30 estimates spanning a full analysis window. Confidence is reported as High, Moderate or Low depending on how much supporting data the neighbouring threshold has.

Refuses to produce a value when: no neighbouring pair agrees, or the estimate comes out at or below zero, which would contradict the assumption that the sensor always consumes some power. In that case the page reports the baseline as unavailable rather than silently substituting zero.

3 · Solar energy collected

Once the darkness baseline is accepted, the solar energy collected is the net rate plus what the system was consuming at the same time:

P_harvest = max(0, P_net + P_drain)The lower limit of zero encodes a physical fact: a solar cell cannot deliver negative power.

Assumption: the system consumes roughly the same power in light and in darkness. This is the weakest link in the chain, and the reason every solar figure on this page is labelled estimated.

What it measures: the power actually delivered into the energy store, not the raw output at the solar cell's terminals, since some power is lost along the way.

4 · How harvest responds to light

A least-squares fit constrained through the origin, since zero light must give zero harvest:

k = Σ(Eᵢ · Pᵢ) ⁄ Σ(Eᵢ²) [µW per lux]E = light level (lux), P = estimated harvest (µW). A single-number summary of the cell's indoor response, not a calibration.

5 · Daily energy

The estimated power is summed over time (trapezoidal integration), split at local midnight so each calendar day gets its fair share:

E = Σ ½·(Pᵢ + Pᵢ₊₁)·(tᵢ₊₁ − tᵢ)Reported in mWh or µWh. Long gaps in the data are skipped, not bridged.

Each day reports its own coverage. A day is marked complete only when more than 95 % of it is covered by data; anything else is labelled a partial day, so a gap can never masquerade as a low-energy day.

6 · Gaps in the charts

Chart lines break rather than draw straight across a long gap (anything longer than max(5 min, 3.5 × sample interval)). A line drawn through an outage would imply measurements that were never taken.

What the setup badge means

The sensor reports what hardware it found and how it is powered. This page enables only what that setup can actually support, and dims the navigation entries it cannot.

ID Setup Available analysis
0Legacy / unknownLive readings, history, tables and CSV export. Solar analysis only if valid storage readings exist.
1Basic sensor + USBInternal chip temperature and supply voltage. No energy store to analyse.
2Basic sensor + external powerInternal chip temperature and supply voltage. Stored energy cannot be measured without the sensor board.
3Sensor board + USBAmbient temperature, humidity and light. Storage readings are turned off while on USB power.
4Sensor board + solar/batteryEverything, including stored energy and solar analysis.

Storage voltage readings are accepted only between 1.5 V and 4.5 V. The sensor, the receiver and this page all apply the same limit, so an implausible reading is rejected at the first stage that sees it.

Built with

The sensor and receiver run on open-source embedded software, the phone link uses Bluetooth, and the plots are drawn with Chart.js. Chart.js is served from this same site, so the page works with no internet connection and contacts no third party.

Privacy

Measurements travel from the receiver to this page over Bluetooth and stay in your browser. The website serves only the page itself and never receives any data. Only your two calculation settings are stored locally, on this device.
